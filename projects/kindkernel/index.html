Multilingual Real-Time Assessment Platform

Research Engineer KindKernel Ltd. Jun 2025 – Mar 2026 Proprietary

The engineering problem

The platform assessed spoken and written language proficiency across three languages in real time, with concurrent learners at scale. The constraint was latency: speech recognition and synthesis had to respond within conversational thresholds on shared GPU infrastructure, while an LLM scoring pipeline graded open-ended spoken responses without contamination from adjacent learner inputs.

What I did

  • Engineered backend systems and optimised GPU-based inference pipelines for real-time speech processing across three languages.
  • Built the LLM-based automated scoring module for open-ended spoken responses.
  • Implemented speech-to-text conversion pipelines for multi-stage spoken-language evaluation.
  • Designed assessment frameworks grounded in educational research.
  • The peer-context contamination problem I encountered here became my AAAI-27 paper.

Deliberate simplicity. The curriculum mapping was straightforward; the difficulty sat in serving real-time multilingual speech at scale on shared GPUs.

Assessment design

  • Assessment rubrics aligned to institutional language proficiency standards.
  • Feedback delivered in the learner's target language.
  • Multi-stage evaluation — staged proficiency assessment rather than correct/incorrect.
  • Grading pipeline designed to isolate individual responses from peer context, which surfaced the contamination problem in my AAAI-27 paper.

Evidence

  • End-to-end platform across three languages (Cantonese, Mandarin, English).
  • Real-time speech processing with GPU-optimised inference.
  • Automated scoring of open-ended spoken responses using LLMs.
  • Multi-stage spoken-language evaluation pipeline.
