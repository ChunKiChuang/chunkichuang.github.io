I study how learners conceptualize and rely on AI systems, alongside counterfactual and mechanistic audits of AI evaluators and explainability tools.

I'm an MPhil researcher at the Faculty of Education, University of Hong Kong. My work bridges human–AI interaction and algorithmic auditing in education—pairing empirical studies of self-regulated learning, feedback-sourced revision, and career-oriented AI conceptions with controlled audits of LLM grading contamination and post-hoc explanation faithfulness. I've published at AIED, with manuscripts in rebuttal at AAAI, in revision for IJAIED, and under review.

I also build the tools I research with. At KindKernel I engineered a voice-based assessment platform (Futurlogue) that placed students in roleplay scenarios and used speech recognition and LLMs to evaluate skills like creativity and problem-solving from their spoken responses. At HKU I built a platform that helps teachers in bilingual programs generate multimodal assessment items using LLMs.

Before the MPhil I studied linguistics at HKU. Outside research, I write fantasy fiction, make pour overs, and walk around cities with no particular destination.
